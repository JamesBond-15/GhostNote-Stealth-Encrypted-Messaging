# GhostNote Privacy Philosophy

GhostNote is built on the belief that privacy is not a feature to be toggled, sold, or compromised — it is the **default state of communication**.

The platform is designed to **reduce reliance on trust, minimize exposure, and minimize dependency** on centralized infrastructure.


---

## Privacy Is the Default
Most modern platforms treat privacy as an optional setting layered on top of data collection.  
GhostNote reverses that model.

Every design decision begins with the assumption that:
- User identity must not be centralized  
- Message content must not be observable  
- Metadata must be minimized  
- Infrastructure must be treated as untrusted  

Privacy is not added later — it is embedded first.

---

## No Personal Identifiers
GhostNote does not require:
- Phone numbers  
- Email addresses  
- Real names  
- Password‑based identity  

User identity exists only as **cryptographic material generated and stored locally** on the device.

---

## Zero‑Trust Infrastructure
Servers are treated only as **message relays**.  
They are not trusted to:
- Hold identity  
- Interpret content  
- Correlate communication  
- Maintain behavioral profiles  

The system is designed to remain safe even if infrastructure is compromised.

---

## User‑Controlled Identity
Users own their identity, not the platform.  

This means:
- No centralized identity recovery  
- No shadow profiles  
- No account graphs  
- No behavioral fingerprinting  

Losing an identity means losing access — not exposing it.

---

## Long‑Term Privacy Commitment
GhostNote is built for **longevity, not virality**.  

This means:
- No advertising dependencies  
- No data monetization  
- No engagement‑based tracking  
- No dark‑pattern onboarding  

The platform evolves without compromising the user’s right to remain unknown.

---

**GhostNote does not chase attention.  
It protects presence.**
